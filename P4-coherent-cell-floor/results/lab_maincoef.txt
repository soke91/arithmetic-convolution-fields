DEPTH 5's RESIDUAL, COMPUTED RATHER THAN NAMED.

An earlier printing of this file explained depth 5's miss as
'1.33 per cent, the size of 1/ell'.  That is the reading the
paper withdraws one paragraph later -- the proposition's term is
O_eta(B^eta/ell), not 1/ell, and reading it as 1/ell sets both the
constant and the divisor sum to 1.  It is also unfalsifiable: with
an unnamed constant no discrepancy could refuse it.  Withdrawn.

The real mechanism is exact and needs no asymptotics.  At depth 5
the cell is ONE residue class mod 2Q, so it is an arithmetic
progression segment N = N_0 + 2Q i.  Every same-cell shift is
h = 2Q k with 0 < |k| < n_c, and every d in the divisor expansion
is coprime to 2Q, so d | h iff d | k.  Hence NO d >= n_c divides
any same-cell shift, while C_T sums over every d.  E_same,5[T] is
therefore deficient against C_T, necessarily downward -- which is
the sign observed.  The deficit is a finite sum over k < n_c.

THE BAND AVERAGE IS EXACTLY 2, AND IT WAS BEING SAMPLED.

  E_all[S_2] = 2C_2 prod_{p>2} [ (1/p)(p-1)/(p-2) + (p-1)/p ]
             = 2C_2 prod_{p>2} (p-1)^2 / (p(p-2)),
  and C_2 = prod_{p>2}(1 - 1/(p-1)^2) = prod_{p>2} p(p-2)/(p-1)^2
  is its reciprocal term by term.  So E_all[S_2] = 2 EXACTLY.

  IT IS A LIMITING VALUE, NOT THE BAND'S OWN AVERAGE.  Taking
  P(p|h) = 1/p for every odd p is what makes the two products
  reciprocal; at a finite band that probability is not exactly
  1/p.  lab_secondcell2.py computes the band's own pair average
  exactly at its band and gets 1.999986107190 -- the difference is the
  band's equidistribution error.  E_{all,B} and E_inf are two
  quantities and {#eq:Dc} subtracts the first.

ROBUSTNESS: is the constant's SPLIT right?  The singular series is
2 C_2 prod_{p|h,p>2}(p-1)/(p-2), and this file cuts that product at
13 -- A below, the Euler factor above.  An off-by-one in which
primes are absorbed into C_2 and which are left in the product
would move the constant by 1/(p(p-2)) at the prime concerned, which
at p = 13 is 1/143 = 0.70 per cent.  Two things say it is not there.

  (a) STRUCTURAL, and it needs no error bar.  A mis-split is a
      UNIFORM multiplicative factor: all six would be off by the
      same amount in the same direction.  The observed ratios fail
      in OPPOSITE directions at the two ends, so no uniform factor
      explains the scatter whatever bound is put on it.

  (b) IN SAMPLING ERRORS, which is the only honest unit here.
      A residual is a point estimate of a sampled quantity and
      cannot bound anything on its own -- reading it as a bound
      is the same defect as N3 above, which read an
      unstandardised statistic while the standardisation was
      printed beside it. So:

      depth  residual %   sampling SE %   a 0.70% mis-split, in SE
      0         0.9190         0.4495               1.6
      1         1.6469         2.8183               0.2
      2         0.1522         0.3295               2.1
      3         0.0211         0.1302               5.4
      4         0.0065         0.0608              11.5
      5         1.3321         0.0051             136.9

      At depth 4 alone a 0.70 per cent mis-split would show at
      11.5 sampling errors, and at depth 3 at 5.4. That is the
      bound, and it is stated in the statistic this file
      already prints rather than in a ratio of percentages.
