  DIAGNOSTIC 1 (post hoc). Why Z4 failed. Past M the layers
  still carry mass -- a few N -- but each one is tiny, so the
  l2 norm that governs a random sign sum is far below it and a
  coin cancels the tail about as well as mu does:
  N            l1 mass at 3000   l2 norm      mu tail   largest
  200000       1.4885            0.0240       0.0002    0.0016
  400000       2.2021            0.0295       0.0082    0.0018
  800000       3.0141            0.0341       0.0008    0.0017
  1600000      3.9771            0.0398       0.0015    0.0020
  So mu earns nothing out there and the refutation is correct:
  the tail is small because the layers are individually small,
  not because of a cancellation only mu can supply. Contrast
  lab_layer_decomposition.py, where the FULL mass is tens of N,
  the l2 norm is dominated by the first layers, and mu beats
  every draw. The cancellation is spent at small m.

  DIAGNOSTIC 3 (post hoc). Why a truncation inside the
  Bombieri-Vinogradov range does not finish the argument.
  L(N;m) is a prime count in the progression p = N (mod m), but
  with the cofactor k = (N-p)/m required SQUAREFREE -- the
  condition mu(k) != 0 that [eq:layers] inherits from
  H(N;k) = mu(k)A(N;k). Dropping it and recomputing the same
  alternating sum over m < 2000:
  N            with mu(k)!=0   without        ratio
  200000       1.7882          2.5644         1.4340
  400000       1.7690          2.5168         1.4227
  800000       1.7730          2.5388         1.4319
  1600000      1.7819          2.5479         1.4299
  The squarefree condition is not decoration: dropping it moves
  the sum by the factor above. Detecting it costs
  mu^2(k) = sum_{d^2 | k} mu(d), which turns each layer into a
  sum over moduli d^2 m with d up to sqrt(N/m) -- and that is
  where the square-root barrier comes back in. The cheap
  truncation in m does not remove it; it relocates it inside
  the layer.
